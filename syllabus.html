Course Description

The course is aimed at studying the patterns and analysis of modern industry, particularly the behavior of firms and the functioning of markets. Global contributions are welcomed in all areas of industrial economics, including: organization of industry; applied oligopoly theory; product differentiation and technical change; theory of the firm and internal organization; and regulation, monopoly, merger, and technology policy.

Course Objectives

  • To provide students with a comprehensive understanding of the behavior of firms and the functioning of markets.
  • To analyze industrial organization and strategies under different market structures.
  • To explore the role of technology, innovation, and product differentiation in shaping modern industries.
  • To understand the impact of regulation, competition policy, and globalization on industries.
  • To develop applied analytical and critical skills for evaluating firm behavior, market outcomes, and policy interventions.

Teaching Philosophy & Learning Outcomes

Teaching Philosophy

Representing the main principles, values, and beliefs that underline the instructor's educational approach:

  • Student-orientation: Built around the needs, interests, and capabilities of students, emphasizing critical thinking, independence, and personal responsibility.
  • Interactivity and active participation: Learning as a dialogue via discussions, projects, role-playing games, and collaborative problem-solving.
  • Connection between theory and practice: Linking academic models directly to real-world economic, social, and technological market dynamics.
  • Ethics and sustainability: Fostering professional social responsibility and an understanding of sustainable development goals.

Expected Learning Outcomes (LOs)

Upon completion of the course, the student will be able to:

  • LO 1: Understand the structure and evolution of industrial sectors globally and nationally.
  • LO 2: Explain the theoretical foundations of the economics of industry markets.
  • LO 3: Analyze the impact of industrial production on GDP growth and employment and analyze the structure and dynamics of key industries.
  • LO 4: Evaluate the challenges of industrial competitiveness in the age of globalization.
  • LO 5: Link industrial development to sustainability and climate goals and develop policy recommendations for industrial modernization.

Course Politics & Ethics

In-Class Ethics & Attendance

To complete the course, you must attend at least 70% of classroom sessions. Course requirements include:

  • Compulsory class attendance and active participation during seminars.
  • Preparation for classes, homework completion, and timely task delivery.
  • Tolerance, openness, friendliness, and constructive feedback in all discussions.
  • Punctuality: Students late to class are not allowed to enter as it disrupts training.

Exam & Absence Policy

Unacceptable behaviors: Unexcused absences, habitual tardiness, and late assignment submissions.

If absent during a control event for a valid, documented reason, you may retake it during IWST hours by prior agreement with the instructor. Otherwise, 0 points will be awarded.

Information & Withdrawal

  • Check your personal Platonus portal and corporate email (@almau.edu.kz) daily for announcements.
  • Academic Withdrawal (AW): Systematic violations of conduct or academic honesty lead to an AW grade, equivalent to an F (GPA 0.0).

Academic Integrity & AI Policy

AlmaU enforces strict standards regarding academic honesty, citation rules, and anti-plagiarism verification across all submitted materials.

Allowed Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI)

  • Automation of complex economic calculations or data modeling.
  • Assisting in understanding core micro/macro economic concepts and formulas.
  • Research assistance, structuring ideas, and self-testing via AI platforms providing feedback.

Prohibited AI Usage & Misconduct

Replacing critical student thinking with AI is strictly forbidden. Generating full written texts, solutions, or essays via ChatGPT without personal intellectual contribution constitutes academic misconduct.

Disciplinary Actions for Violations

  1. Warning & Explanation: Issued for minor, first-time unconscious violations.
  2. Resubmission: Allowed under strict academic standards for non-serious infractions.
  3. Grade Reduction / Cancellation: Direct zero/penalty on affected assignments.
  4. Academic Investigation: Severe cheating/plagiarism triggers formal review, leading to course failure with an F grade or administrative withdrawal.

Evaluation Policy & Grading Scale

Academic performance is evaluated on a 100-point scale. The final course grade is calculated as follows:

Final Grade = Rating × 0.6 + Final Exam × 0.4

Where Rating = (RK1 + RK2) / 2. RK1 and RK2 represent the average arithmetic scores of current performance and midterm controls in the first and second halves of the term.
