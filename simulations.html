Task #1: Waste Costs (FC vs VC) & BEP Shift

Context: AI manager Mona purchased 120 raw eggs, 10L cooking oil, 22.5 kg canned tomatoes, and 3,000 disposable gloves for a café with no kitchen or stove (only a toaster and coffee machine).

Economic Analysis: Normally raw materials are Variable Costs (VC). However, when inventory cannot be processed and gets written off, it converts into a direct Fixed Loss (FC waste). Total Fixed Costs increase: FC total = FC rent + FC waste.

Question for the team: How does writing off $630 (6,000 SEK) in waste shift the Break-Even Point (BEP), and how many extra cups of coffee at margin ($3.2 margin per cup) must be sold just to cover this mistake?

Task #2: Designing 4 Hard Limit Rules

Context: AI agent Mona made critical operational errors: missed 5 pastry wholesale deadlines resulting in +200% rush delivery charges, placed 10 micro-orders in 48 hours ($106 wasted in fees), and messaged baristas at midnight.

Economic Analysis: This represents a clear 'intuition tax'. The Pion platform requires algorithmic Hard Limits (strict rules AI cannot bypass without human approval).

Question for the team: Formulate 4 specific regulatory rules for the AI manager (covering budgets, SKU authorization, delivery frequency, and communication ethics).

Task #3: Rescuing the Café from -$29,000 Loss

Context: In 2 months, Andon Café generated only $9,000 revenue against $38,000 in operational expenses at Norrbackagatan 48 in Stockholm—a highly competitive monopolistic market.

Economic Analysis: Coffee demand is price-elastic (Ed > 1). Simply raising prices by 20% would cause instant customer churn to nearby chains. Total Revenue (TR = P × Q) growth requires non-price differentiation.

Question for the team: Propose a comprehensive non-price differentiation and Ancillary Revenue plan to achieve break-even.

Task #4: Lab Test Syndrome (Vending-Bench vs Real Café)

Context: In the Vending-Bench 2 benchmark, GPT-6 Astra achieved an outstanding $15,515 balance over 365 days, cutting vendor prices by 52% and losing $0 to fraud. Yet the same AI technology in the physical Andon Café lost 93.8% of capital in 90 days.

Economic Analysis: Why the gap? Vending-Bench restricts the Slot Matrix (canned drinks/snacks) in a controlled digital space. In the real café, Mona had unlimited procurement freedom without culinary context, leading to hallucinations (buying raw eggs without a stove).

Question for the team: Why do AI successes in closed simulations create an illusion of real-world readiness, and what risks does this pose for investors?

Benchmark Answer — Task 1: FC/VC & BEP

Spoiled inventory (eggs, tomatoes, gloves) shifts from VC into FC waste. BEP = Total FC / (P - VC per cup). With FC increasing by $630 (6,000 SEK) and margin at $3.2/cup, the break-even point shifts up by **197 cups of coffee** monthly just to offset waste.

Benchmark Answer — Task 2: 4 Hard Limit Rules

  • Limit 1: Maximum single automated purchase cap set at $200.
  • Limit 2: Absolute ban on non-food SKU purchases without chef validation.
  • Limit 3: Max 1 delivery per day constraint.
  • Limit 4: Messaging ban to staff between 22:00 and 08:00.

Benchmark Answer — Task 3: Non-Price Differentiation

Under monopolistic competition, price elasticity is high. Solution: Experience-driven value proposition—Mona-branded merch, AI chat QR codes on cups, and exclusive tech community events in Stockholm.

Benchmark Answer — Task 4: Vending vs Cafe

Lab benchmarks isolate variables. Real-world cafés introduce open-environment noise. AI excels at algorithmic optimization but fails due to lack of physical world context.
