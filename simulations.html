Analytical Context by Decade:
  • 1970-1980: Western economies peak in industrial output. US and Germany dominate global manufacturing.
  • 1990: Post-Soviet restructuring. Note on Kazakhstan: Pre-1991 data is retroactively estimated by the World Bank based on the Kazakh SSR's share of Soviet Net Material Product (heavy raw materials & extractive metallurgy vs. RSFSR military-industrial complex).
  • 2000-2010: China enters the WTO (2001), causing an explosive manufacturing boom and draining industrial share from the US and UK.
  • 2020: Western economies transition to service and knowledge-intensive sectors, while Asia remains the primary industrial engine.

US Civil War (1861–1865) & The Birth of Standard Sizing (S/M/L)

To clothe 2 million Union soldiers, the US military collected mass body measurements, inventing standard ready-to-wear sizing grids (S, M, L). This abolished custom tailoring for masses, creating modern garment manufacturing. Source: History of Clothing Sizes.

1960 – 2000

Third Industrial Revolution: IT, Microprocessors & Automation

Moore's Law & Computing Cost Deflation (1970–2000)

Chart Explanations:

  • Transistor Count Index: Represents Moore's Law (doubling every 2 years), causing hardware costs to collapse.
  • MFG Productivity Index: Manufacturing output per worker hour, which surged as computers optimized factory workflows.

USA vs. Japan (1980s): While the US dominated software and microprocessor architecture (Intel, Microsoft), Japan led the physical shop-floor implementation of Industry 3.0 via Lean Manufacturing and CNC robotics (Fanuc, Toyota), disrupting global automotive markets with high quality and low costs.
