1760 – 1840

First Industrial Revolution (Industry 1.0)

Mechanization, Watt's Steam Engine, Wilkinson's Boring Machine & Textile Production Boom.

1774 Engineering Breakthrough: John Wilkinson's Boring Machine & Watt's Engine

Before 1774, cannons and cylinders were cast around clay cores, resulting in uneven bores. Gas leaked, and early Newcomen engines had a dismal 1% efficiency. John Wilkinson invented a machine that bored solid iron cylinders with high precision. James Watt used this to prevent steam leaks, boosting thermodynamic efficiency by 400%!

5.0mm = Newcomen Engine (1712) | 0.5mm = Wilkinson/Watt Precision (1775)

Lancashire Textile Boom (1760–1840): Labor Productivity Surge

Hargreaves' Spinning Jenny (1764) and Arkwright's Water Frame reduced spinning labor time from 500 hours to 3 hours per kg of cotton yarn, crashing prices by 90%.

1870 – 1914

Second Industrial Revolution: Mass Production

Standardization, S/M/L Sizing Grid, Bessemer Steel & Ford Assembly Line Economics.

US Civil War (1861–1865) & The Birth of Standardized Clothing Sizes (S/M/L)

To clothe 2 million Union soldiers, the US military collected physical measurements, inventing standard ready-to-wear sizing grids (S, M, L). This abolished custom tailoring for masses, creating the modern garment manufacturing industry.

Ford Model T Moving Assembly Line Simulator (1913)

Moving assembly line reduced chassis assembly time from 12.5h to 1.5h per car. Fixed costs were spread over 2 million units, dropping retail price from $850 to $260!

1960 – 2000

Third Industrial Revolution: Automation & IT

Microprocessors, Moore's Law, PLCs and Automotive Industrial Robotics (Unimate).

Moore's Law Impact

Transistor density doubling every 2 years caused computing costs to collapse, enabling real-time programmable logic controllers (PLCs) on factory floors.

Robotics ROI

First Unimate robot at General Motors (1961) reduced welding defect rates by 85% and eliminated hazardous manual labor in die casting.
