Analytical Context by Decade:
  • 1970-1980: Western economies peak in industrial output. The US and Germany dominate global manufacturing.
  • 1990: Post-Soviet collapse. Note on Kazakhstan: Pre-1991 data is retroactively estimated by the World Bank based on the Kazakh SSR's share of the Soviet Net Material Product. It differs from Russia due to Kazakhstan's heavy focus on extractive metallurgy and raw materials vs. Russia's military-industrial complex.
  • 2000-2010: China enters the WTO (2001), causing an explosive manufacturing boom, draining industrial share from the US and UK (Deindustrialization).
  • 2020: Western economies transition fully to service/knowledge economies, while Asia remains the industrial hub.

Macro Comparison: GDP per Capita & Industrial Growth (1760–1840)

Why did empires diverge? The UK embraced mechanization (textiles, steam), driving GDP up. Tsarist Russia and the Ottoman Empire relied on agrarian serfdom and manual guild labor, resulting in economic stagnation and a widening technological gap.

US Civil War (1861–1865) & Standard Sizing (S/M/L)

To clothe 2 million Union soldiers rapidly, the US military collected mass physical measurements, inventing the first standard ready-to-wear sizing grids (S, M, L). This abolished custom tailoring, birthing modern mass garment manufacturing. Source: History of Clothing Sizes.

Moore's Law & Computing Deflation (1970–2000)

Chart Explanations:

  • Transistor Count Index: Represents Moore's Law (transistors doubling every 2 years), leading to a collapse in hardware costs.
  • MFG Productivity Index: Manufacturing output per worker hour, which skyrocketed as computers replaced manual calculation.

USA vs. Japan (1980s): While the US dominated PC microprocessors and software design (Intel, Microsoft), Japan led the physical implementation of Industry 3.0 via Lean Manufacturing and CNC (Computer Numerical Control) robotics (Fanuc, Toyota). Japan's automation allowed them to produce higher quality cars at lower costs, heavily disrupting the US auto market.
